> Have you had any personal experience with lucid dreaming?

### Professors Fan and Tan:

> Once I had a bad dream where someone was chasing me in the middle of the night. In the dream, I realized it was just a dream and continued talking to myself to find a way out. Somehow, I managed to take control of my body and started moving my hands, then my legs, and finally my whole body until I woke up. In the dream, I ran to a neighboring house, hid behind a couch, and waited for the police to arrive. When they finally came, it was already dawn outside, and bright white light flooded the room. I felt incredibly grateful to be alive, and this experience deeply touched me.

### Professor Tan:

> I can't remember a lucid dream, but I had several pre-lucid dreams. In them, I recognized certain signs of dreaming, such as flying in the sky or seeing a person with a goat's face speaking, and I realized that I was dreaming. I didn't feel the need to control the dream, so I just let the story unfold.

### Professors Fan and Tan:

> This was the final phase of research under the guidance of Denis Banchenko. For two months, our team has been performing a series of practices aimed at stimulating the process of lucid dreaming. At that time, some progress in overcoming the 'First Gates' could already be seen. One of the characteristics of the influence of these practices is the increase in the brightness and duration of the realism of dreams. Even if you lack the energy to become aware in dreams, you can still experience a completely unique and vivid experience, as it happened to me.
> The nature of dreams changes completely as the "gates of the first dream" approach. These are not the dreams shown in movies, where everything around resembles the futuristic world of "Alice in Wonderland". These dreams are so real that sometimes they seem much more real than our usual life. Sounds, smells, sensations, tastes - they are so vivid that you get fully immersed in the world you see in your dream. Millions of sensations: from the rustling of sand under your feet and the wind in your hair, to the warmth of a sun-heated stone when touched and the moist-filled air. Anyone who has ever meditated in nature will understand the depth of perception of the surrounding world that I am talking about.

## Lucid dreaming can be a powerful tool for improving memory.

### ASRP.media

> How do lucid dreams, in your opinion, affect a person's memory and attention in everyday life?

### Professors Fan and Tan:

> If the content of lucid dreams is not controlled with a specific purpose, they can only affect the duration of attention concentration. Considering that many lucid dreamers derive satisfaction and relaxation from lucid dreams, however, if lucid dreams are induced with a specific purpose, such as for test rehearsal, we believe they can be a powerful tool for improving memory. Therefore, the key to lucid dreaming is to have and maintain a clear goal in lucid dreaming.

### ASRP.media

> In the description of your interests on the ResearchGate website, you mentioned the clinical application of lucid dreaming and brain-computer interaction. Could you please elaborate on these topics? In your opinion, what positive changes can lucid dreaming bring to medicine and computer technologies?

### Professors Fan and Tan:

> In recent studies, artificial intelligence and fMRI have been used to transform participants' dreams into real images or frames. We are particularly intrigued by the possibility of combining this technology or brain-computer interface based on imagined movements with lucid dreaming. We believe that such a combination can make a significant contribution to our understanding of human brain activity and advance the development of artificial limbs and virtual reality technologies.

### ASRP.media

> What is the biggest problem you see in the research of lucid dreaming today?

### Professors Fan and Tan:

> Attempting to explain the sensation of being in a lucid dream is akin to describing colors to someone with color blindness. That's exactly how we feel when we read reports from lucid dreamers or try to explain lucid dreaming to those unfamiliar with this realm. As noted by researchers like Stephen LaBerge, lucid dreaming requires effort. It may not be an easy task for many. However, if we want to fully explore the potential of lucid dreaming, we will need a much larger number of participants in our research than we currently have. Thus, the biggest challenge we face is the difficulty in inducing lucid dreams and maintaining lucidity, which limits the size of our sample and hinders progress in our studies.
