## Lucid dreaming in Freud's theory and psychoanalysis.

The most important milestone in the history of dream research was the era of Sigmund Freud in the early 20th century. Freud developed the theory that dreams are the 'royal road to the unconscious' and proposed methods of dream analysis to uncover hidden desires and conflicts in the human mind. He also identified dreams as a means of expressing and processing emotional experiences.

## Steven LaBerge and the development of lucid dreaming techniques.

Significant progress in the study of lucid dreaming was only achieved in the late 20th century. Stephen LaBerge, an American psychologist, is considered one of the first researchers to systematically explore this field in the 1980s. He developed methodologies and techniques that allow individuals to actively participate in their dreams and even control their content. Practices such as reality checks throughout the day helped people become aware of their dreams and become active participants in their own dream experiences. (Read more about this in the article 'Stephen LaBerge: Leading Researcher in the World of Lucid Dreaming')

## Neurobiology, lucid dreaming, and modern research.

With the development of modern neuroscientific methods and technologies, such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electroencephalography (EEG), researchers have begun to gain a deeper understanding of the biological and neurological mechanisms of lucid dreaming. (More about the methods of studying lucid dreaming is discussed in the article 'Lucid Dreaming: Neurophysiological Research Methods'). One key finding is that lucid dreaming is characterized by the activation of specific brain regions, confirming their distinction from ordinary dreams and their genuine nature.

## Modern research on lucid dreaming and its practical application.

Currently, scientific research in the field of lucid dreaming is actively developing. These studies cover a wide range of topics, from exploring methods of lucid dream stimulation to their practical application and the economic prospects of the lucid dreaming market. One such example is the use of lucid dreaming as a tool for treating post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and developing self-improvement skills.

## Science and Dreams. What to expect in the future?

The history of the development of science in the field of lucid dreaming emphasizes that this amazing aspect of human consciousness has been of interest to researchers for many centuries. With the development of modern methods and technologies, our ability to be aware of and control our own dreams has become more accessible and understandable. This opens up new horizons for a deeper understanding of dreams and their impact on humans.
