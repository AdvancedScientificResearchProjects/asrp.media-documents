> Anna, tell us how you got involved in the research and how you managed to combine the intensive two-month course on lucid dreams with your busy daily life in the capital city?

### Anna Budanova:

> In 2020, before the pandemic, I attended an offline seminar by Denis Banchenko dedicated to extrasensory perception. I liked the systematic and scientific approach to topics that often go beyond the boundaries of logic and science. Therefore, in October 2022, when enrollment for participation in a scientific research within the "Art of Lucid Dreaming" course was announced, I immediately signed up. These were, if I may put it this way, "field training" in real-life conditions, while having a job, social contacts, and responsibilities. Setting up inner discipline regarding daily routines, diet, and practicing became a real challenge. With only 24 hours a day, 8 of which are allocated to sleep, you must truly prioritize and discard everything unnecessary to achieve results. Easier said than done, habits and your environment will pull you back into old patterns. Yes, maintaining a routine is difficult, yes, there will be setbacks, but it's not a sprint; it's a marathon for a lifetime. You can and should always return to your chosen path. Yes, your surroundings may not understand why you gave up a glass of champagne or left an event early to go to sleep before 10:00 PM. Asserting your boundaries and the value of the self-improvement work you are doing is a thankless and energy-consuming task. So, if you're unlucky with your environment, sooner or later, it may prevail, unless, of course, you start cutting toxic ties and choose yourself. But be prepared that you may not be forgiven for it. This is precisely the moment when ruthless prioritization and separation of your own path from the imposed one are essential. In this regard, I really liked the practice of "Am I doing nonsense?". It is very sobering and allows you to reconsider your daily routine and, yes, life in general. Perhaps for me, it became a stimulus for radical changes.

### ASRP.media:

> What changes occurred in your habits, perception, dreams, and how did the practices of the "Banchenko Algorithm" methodology affect your quality of life in the long run?

### Anna Budanova:

> During the research and the practice of techniques, life was rapidly changing, as well as my perception of the world and myself in it. The nature of dreams was changing as well; they became more vivid, narrative, and longer. However, crossing the barrier of self-awareness in a dream was quite challenging. The reason is that in our daily lives, we don't subject the surrounding world to reality checks; it is real for us by default. Therefore, even in the most fantastic dream, we do not pass what's happening through a filter of criticality. Developing the habit of analyzing the surrounding world, performing reality checks, gradually transfers to our dreams, leading to a new stage - the state preceding lucid dreams (LD). In this state, you already understand that the world around you is unreal, but you cannot yet realize that it is a dream. However, in this state, you can already notice that the physical laws of dream worlds differ from what you are used to. And that is incredible!
> When you realize that what is happening is a dream, you become like a superhero with heightened sensory perception of the world. Everything around becomes brighter and clearer. You gain access to experiences that are practically impossible in real life. Ever wanted to levitate, move objects with your mind, or walk through a mirror? If you've ever been on a roller coaster, you might remember the unexplained joy and excitement it brought. Well, the emotions you experience upon waking up are much stronger. You wake up full of energy and in a great mood.
> Perhaps the main result of my participation in the research was a complete reevaluation of life priorities. A lot of unnecessary, false, and superfluous thoughts and habits disappeared. Ordinary dreams became more vivid, narrative, and longer, allowing you to save money on movie tickets. Completely new categories of dreams emerged, with reality splitting into multiple layers. I developed my own markers for entering a lucid dream; for example, almost every time I see a beetle or a cockroach in a dream, I think it's the "reality-bug." At that moment, it becomes pixelated, and I realize I'm dreaming. The same happens when I see text in a dream. I remember my first LD during the course; I was flipping through a chronicle, and the letters started to blur and dance. When I tried to focus and examine them, they began to glow, and I realized. The excitement was so intense that I was almost thrown out of the dream, but subsequent LDs became longer. My lifestyle changed, I became calmer and healthier. A lot of excess, unnecessary, and imposed things disappeared from my life. And, of course, every time after a lucid dream, there is an incredible surge of energy and inspiration. I was charmed by it.

These dreams are so real that at times they seem much more real than our familiar life. The sounds, smells, sensations, and tastes are so vivid that you get fully immersed in the world you see in the dream. Millions of sensations, from the rustling of sand under your feet and the wind in your hair to the warmth of the sun-heated stone upon touch and the moisture-filled air. Anyone who has ever practiced meditation in nature will understand the depth of perception of the surrounding world that I'm talking about.

## Unusual Dream Narrated by Anna:

By that time, our group had become quite close-knit. Dima, one of the team members, contacted me. Denis, our mentor, told him about an unusual place of power that he had visited before, and we decided to go there together. By the way, places of power are locations with special energy that can influence us and our perception of reality. In simple terms, places of power can be temples, iconic locations, unique natural anomalies, or, as Castaneda would describe them, places capable of shifting our assemblage point under certain conditions. And that's precisely what is needed to master the art of lucid dreaming.We met early in the morning with the first rays of dawn. The small town didn't look particularly lively even during the day, and now, it gave off an eerie post-apocalyptic impression. Do you remember the first scenes of "28 Days Later" when the protagonist stepped out onto the deserted city streets? I think his impressions were similar to ours.Along the street with worn asphalt, long strips of gray concrete buildings with balconies along the facades stretched out – a gloomy legacy of the constructivists. The dark shop windows on the ground floors, some of which were closed with shutters, evoked feelings of confinement and melancholy. What can I add? Morning is not my time, and expecting cheerful associations from me before the first cup of coffee and at least until 10 in the morning is a completely failed idea. It couldn't be said about my comrade, who eagerly, with a skip in his step, constantly wanted to move forward, pushing me every time I, yawning, got lost in thought while looking at the buildings around. He couldn't wait to quickly climb the rocky ridge located on the outskirts of the old town, separating it from a small new district. To be honest, this irritated me considerably. In such early hours, I was simply incapable of sharing the joy of upcoming physical activity, feeling more like one of Edgar Allan Poe's characters seeking doom or Stoker's Dracula looking for a coffin to hide from the outside world and immerse myself in sweet slumber. However, the "die is cast," and once a decision is made, doubts are out of place, and therefore, annoyance and complaining are as well. So, I just resigned myself to it. We approached the ridge, which resembled an elongated, petrified fin of a shark made of red rocky rock, almost separating the old town from a small new district. However, it wasn't very high, although in some places it had almost sheer slopes. A narrow path zigzagged up the hill, and upon reaching the summit, it ran along the entire ridge. Nothing unusual: a mixture of stones, sand, and dry earth with occasional protruding rocky outcrops, and, infrequently, withered grass. It was impossible for two people to walk side by side on such a path, so I followed Dima. But despite his large hiking backpack and sandals that were completely unsuitable for such hikes, the distance between us occasionally increased, and he had to turn around and wait for me as I, breathing heavily, struggled to catch up. Honestly, I would have given up on all of this a long time ago if it weren't for the conversation with the Shadow. The Shadow is the voice-guide, occasionally taking on the blurry outlines of a tall male shadow standing nearby, providing answers to any questions. Or, as Carlos Castaneda called it in "The Art of Dreaming" – the Emissary. Oh, I forgot to mention, all of this happened in a dream; at that time, I still didn't know that.One of the peculiarities of the Emissaries is that they always tell the truth, but how we interpret it depends on us. I asked the Shadow how it would be when we reached the place of power.Will I enter a lucid dream afterward, falling asleep at home, or do I need to fall asleep right on the mountain?You will enter a lucid dream immediately; you won't need to fall asleep. Your assemblage point will shift, and you will become aware in the dream.I didn't quite understand what "becoming aware in the dream immediately" meant. At that moment, the world around me was entirely real, and I couldn't understand how one could become aware in a dream without falling asleep. Although, it seemed that the hint was right on the surface, in dreams, it's very difficult to overcome the barrier of rationality that tells us that everything we see is real, even though, of course, it's not.So, I'll just become aware while staying here?Yes.Is it something like hallucinations? So, my brain will start generating alpha waves as in the rapid eye movement (REM) sleep phase, and I will be awake but lucid dreaming?You won't be awake; you will become aware in the dream.By that time, I had fallen quite far behind, and, engrossed in the conversation, I withdrew into myself, not noticing that we were already at the top of the ridge. A distant cry brought me back to reality: "Come on! We're close!"I looked around. The city was visible somewhere below, and ahead was an endless clear sky and the silhouettes of hills. The realization of how high we were struck me abruptly, and something resembling a panic attack began. The air trembled, everything around seemed to get closer and farther away, completely erasing any sense of distances and scale, disorienting me entirely. My head buzzed, and my heart felt like it was about to burst out of my chest. I stopped, trying to concentrate.Keep going, don't stop, - advised the Emissary.I can't! I'm scared! I can't feel the dimensions; everything is unreal, - I replied, slowly squatting and trying to feel the ground to sense some stability and monumentality beneath my feet.It's a regular assemblage point shift; you just need to keep going, - the voice said emotionlessly.I can't. I really want to sleep. I just need a little nap...I began to be drawn into sleep, my eyes stuck together and filled with heaviness. I gradually lay down on the path, repeating that I needed to sleep.You don't need to sleep; you need to keep going. You'll become aware soon, and the fear will pass.By that time, I was crawling. Losing the last sense of reality and unable to resist the desire to sleep, I lay down on the path, closed my eyes, and sank into another dream.I felt on my own skin what it's like when the assemblage point shifts abruptly, and you realize that the world is much more malleable than we imagine, and what we see is not the result of an unchanging environment but the result of our rigid perceptions of it fixed in one position. I often remember this dream, and every time, I ask myself the same question: was that place actually real?Upon waking up, I immediately recorded this dream and made sketches of the place. When I shared them with the team, the idea arose to check if this place exists in reality.
